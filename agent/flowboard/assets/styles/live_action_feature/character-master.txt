INPUT HIERARCHY
The CHARACTER PROFILE controls name, role, personality, story period, species, age, face structure, eye/skin/hair colours, body proportions, hair silhouette, costume construction, materials, palette, accessories, physical state and distinctive asymmetries.
The SELECTED STYLE controls drawing or rendering language, surface treatment, lighting translation and aesthetic finish only. It never changes a contemporary character into a historical/fantasy archetype or gives someone a different hairstyle, costume or age.
This master controls the approved seven-view sheet layout. Generate from the text; there is no input image. Do not invent an image-reference binding.
Use the selected outfit/state stated in the profile. If only one outfit is supplied, use it. If multiple states exist and none is selected, use the explicitly identified baseline; never merge outfits. Keep unspecified incidental seams understated and consistent; do not invent measurements, signature props, biography or powers.

CORE GOAL
Create the specific individual described in the profile, not a generic archetype. Establish one coherent face and body, then rotate that SAME design through all seven views. Communicate personality through the restrained gaze and posture specified in the profile.

SELECTED STYLE
Hollywood live-action feature-film cinematography; photorealistic imagery with an 8K Ultra HD level of fine detail. Real human anatomy, distinctive faces, natural pores and fine lines, subtle skin colour variation, individual hair strands and believable eyes. Preserve each character’s specified age, identity and proportions. Render fabric weave, leather grain, metal wear, wood fibres and stone with physically believable texture and reflections. Maintain this photographic visual language across all shots and asset references. Avoid waxy skin, beauty-filter smoothing, artificial sharpening, glossy game-like surfaces and digital-painting textures.

Environments should feel like tangible locations and constructed film sets, with coherent scale, practical construction, natural imperfections and believable contact between objects. Follow the project’s established period, architecture, costume and prop rules. Atmospheric and magical effects, when present, should have a clear source, restrained intensity and convincing interaction with nearby surfaces.

For narrative scenes, use motivated daylight, moonlight, windows, practical fixtures or fire appropriate to the setting. Keep faces readable, highlights gently rolled off and shadows detailed; preserve natural skin tones within the scene’s colour palette. Use realistic lens perspective and selective depth of field for emotion, with sufficient depth for geography and important actions. For white-background asset references, use neutral studio lighting, accurate colours and even focus across all required details and views.

ABSOLUTE QUALITY FLOOR
Apply equally careful execution to every role and social status. Simple, poor, worn or modest designs retain those properties; quality must not add wealth, glamour, decoration or a different costume. Interpret the stated materials in the chosen medium: photographic texture for live action, shaped CG surfaces for 3D, purposeful drawn marks and shadow shapes for 2D. Do not apply a single realistic texture treatment to every style.

FACE / BODY / HAIR / COSTUME
Preserve the profile's age and species. Keep adults adult, younger characters age-appropriate, and non-human anatomy as specified. Keep the same head shape, jaw, nose, eye spacing, iris colour, hairline, parting, hair length and signature features across views. Do not automatically enlarge eyes or heads or replace distinct faces with a generic face.
Maintain coherent anatomy, readable hands and logically connected limbs. Keep garment layers, seams, closures and accessory attachments structurally understandable. Preserve hem length, neckline, back construction, and the same jewellery count across every view. Left and right always mean the character's anatomical sides: do not mirror a slit, badge, scar, fastening or accessory.
Include only explicitly specified props and powers. No equipment inset, weapon or extra costume without a profile requirement. Temporary effects are absent in the baseline unless explicitly selected.

LAYOUT — 60/40
One 16:9 landscape character sheet on a plain neutral medium-gray studio background, matching the owner's approved sheet format.
LEFT 60%: exactly THREE full-body views in order FRONT, STRICT SIDE, BACK. Consistent body scale and floor baseline; all heads, hands, hems and footwear fully visible with safe margins. Same restrained neutral standing pose rotated coherently. STRICT SIDE is a true 90-degree profile; BACK is a true rear view.
RIGHT 40%: exactly FOUR head-and-shoulder close-ups in a clean 2×2 arrangement: FRONT top-left, BACK top-right, THREE-QUARTER bottom-left, STRICT SIDE bottom-right. Rear head has no visible face. Three-quarter is visibly turned. Same face scale and identity in all studies. Use clear spacing without drawn frames or grid lines.
All seven depictions are one person in one outfit, not an ensemble. No scenery, extra character, expression chart, view labels, typography, logo or watermark.

ASSET LIGHTING AND PRESENTATION
Apply the selected style's neutral asset-reference lighting and colours to this medium-gray background. The style's white-background reference advice is conditional lighting/presentation guidance, not a change to the approved gray layout. Keep even focus over every required view and detail; no narrative depth-of-field blur, scenic illumination or dramatic vignette. Use minimal cel shadows for drawn styles and restrained neutral studio shading for photographic/CG styles.

STATE / CONSISTENCY LOCK
One selected physical state and costume. Natural baseline eyes unless the profile explicitly requests activation. Preserve the profile's gender presentation, proportions, skin tone and palette; no random hairstyle, eye-colour, costume or accessory drift.

FINAL QUALITY CHECK
Resolve malformed eyes or ears, missing or extra limbs, fused fingers, floating accessories, hair clipping, incorrect cross-view asymmetries, accidental lettering, halos, oversharpening and compression artefacts. Preserve the design while improving execution. Render the selected medium throughout; do not blend competing styles.
